# New Codes from Cyclic and Negacyclic Codes of Even Length over Z4 ∗

## Nuh Aydin Mohamed O. Belghith Godwin Idowu Trang T.T. Nguyen Long B. Tran Kenyon College, Gambier OH 43022, USA 

Abstract 

This paper uses theoretical results previously established in the literature to design search algorithms to find new linear codes over Z4 from cyclic and negacyclic codes of even length. As a result of these searches, we have found 2500 new cyclic codes and 730 negacyclic codes. These new codes exhibit improved parameters compared to previously known codes. Additionally, we have obtained binary quantum codes with good parameters from such Z4 codes. 

Keywords: Cyclic codes, Constacyclic, Quasi-cyclic codes, Quasitwisted cyclic codes 

MSC codes: 68Q25, 68R10, 68U05 

# 1 Introduction and Motivation 

After the discovery that some good non-linear binary codes can be obtained as images of Z4-linear codes (also called quaternary codes in the literature) in [15], research on codes over Z4 and codes over many other finite rings exploded. Codes over rings became a prominent topic in algebraic coding theory. Given the influence of the publication [15], much subsequent research on codes over 

Z4, and the fact that many of the rings studied are extensions of Z4, codes over Z4 attained a prominent place in algebraic coding theory. As a result, a database of best known codes over Z4

was established [5], similar to the one on linear codes over fields [14]. The initial database [5] was relocated and expanded in a recent work [6]. In the process of updating the database, researchers found thousands of new linear codes over Z4 from cyclic codes of odd length, and quasi-cyclic (QC) codes that used those cyclic codes as building blocks (or seeds). This work is a continuation of the work done in [6]. In this work, our aim is to expand the earlier work and to find more Z4-linear codes with better parameters for the database. We conducted computer searches on cyclic codes of even length over Z4, both free and non free, as well as negacyclic codes of even length. Addi-tionally, we also constructed binary quantum codes derived from cyclic and negacyclic codes over Z4.The paper is organized as follows. We start with some basic facts and definitions in section 2 about codes over Z4. Then, we summarize basic facts about cyclic codes and some of their general-izations in section 3. We explain the theory we use in producing the algorithms in section 4. Finally, we present the results of computer searches in section 5.

Given these computational complexity challenges, researchers often focus on promising subclasses of linear codes with rich mathematical structures. 

# 3 Cyclic Codes and Their Generalizations 

Cyclic codes and their various generalizations play a key role in algebraic coding theory. Their structure makes them easier to implement in practice, and they establish a key link between algebra and coding theory. 

Definition 3.1. A code C of length n over a finite field Fq is called a cyclic code if for every codeword 

v = ( v0, v 1, . . . , v n−1) ∈ C , the word obtained by cyclically shifting v by one position to the right, that is, σ(v) = ( vn−1, v 0, v 1, . . . , v n−2), is also in C.

One of the useful generalizations of cyclic codes are quasi-cyclic (QC) codes. Those linear codes which are invariant under a cyclic shift by some fixed number of positions are quasi-cyclic. 

Definition 3.2. A code linear C of length n over a finite ring or a field is called a quasi-cyclic code of index ℓ if for every codeword v = ( v0, v 1, . . . , v n−1) ∈ C , the word obtained by cyclically shifting v

by ℓ positions, that is, σℓ(v) = ( vn−ℓ, v n−ℓ+1 , . . . , v n−1, v 0, v 1, . . . , v n−ℓ−1), is also in C.

Definition 3.3. A linear code of length N over a ring R is constacyclic if for some unit a ∈ R, the code is invariant under the automorphism σa defined by 

(c0, c 1, . . . , c N −1) 7 → (ac N −1, c 0, c 1, . . . , c N −2).

Note that the special case a = 1 gives cyclic codes. In the case a = −1, we say that the code is 

negacyclic . Constacyclic codes of length N over R can be identified as ideals in the quotient ring 

R[x]/⟨xN − a⟩ via the isomorphism from RN to R[x]/⟨xN − a⟩ defined by 

(v0, v 1, . . . , v N −1) 7 → v0 + v1x + · · · + vN −1xN −1.

The algebraic structure of cyclic and constacyclic codes over a finite field Fq is quite simple. They are ideals in the quotient ring Fq [x]/⟨xN − a⟩ which is a principal ideal ring. For every constacyclic code 

C of length N over Fq , there is a unique monic polynomial g(x) of least degree in C that generates 

C, i.e., C = ⟨g(x)⟩ = {f (x)g(x) mod xN − a : f (x) ∈ Fq [x]}. This polynomial is called the standard generator polynomial of C and it divides xN −a. Hence, there is a one-to-one correspondence between constacyclic codes of length N over Fq and divisors of xN − a over Fq . When gcd( N, q ) = 1 , the polynomial xN − a has N distinct roots (in an extension of Fq ), otherwise it has repeated roots. In either case, all constacyclic codes of length N over Fq can be obtained from the divisors of xN − a.

# 4 Cyclic Codes of Even Length over Z4

When we consider cyclic codes over Z4, their algebraic structure is more complicated. In the case 

gcd( N, 4) = 1 , that is when N is odd, their structure is similar to the case of cyclic codes over Fq .This is the easier case where the factorization of xN − 1 into irreducibles over Z4 is unique and can be obtained from the factorization of xN − 1 into irreducibles over Z2 via the Hensel lift [17]. Let 3N be the length of a Z4-linear code. It can uniquely be written as N = 2 k · n where n is odd. We can classify the codes in Z4 based on the parity of code length N . If k = 0 then N = n is odd. This is the case considered in [6] where the authors found a large number of new Z4-linear codes from cyclic codes of odd length over Z4. The structure of cyclic codes of odd length over Z4 is given by the following theorem: 

Theorem 4.1 ([8]) . Let C be a cyclic code in Rn = Z4[x]/⟨xn − 1⟩ where n is odd. Then, Rn is a principal ideal ring and 

C = ⟨(g(x), 2a(x)) ⟩ = ⟨g(x) + 2 a(x)⟩

where g(x), a(x) are polynomials with a(x) | g(x) | (xn − 1) mod 4 .

If k ≥ 1 (N = 2 k · n as above), then N is even. We consider two subcases: 

• If k = 1 , then N is said to be oddly even, that is, N is a multiple of 2 and not 4. 

• If k > 1, then N is a multiple of 4. The first theorem for which we implemented a search algorithm only handles the oddly even case:

## 4.4 Quantum codes 

Building large scale quantum computers is a very active area of research. A critical part of this effort is the ability to control quantum errors. There are various methods of constructing quantum error correcting codes (QECC) over finite fields Fq , many of them based on the CSS construction [12]. Codes over extension rings of Fq have been utilized to construct QECCs over Fq . A large number of works are available in the literature on this approach. Recently, some binary QECCs have been obtained from the extension rings of Z4 in [13] and [3]. One of our goals in this work was to obtain some binary quantum codes from codes over Z4, rather than from codes over an extension ring of 

Z4 as done in the recent papers. We used a method similar [13] and [3]. Additionally, we examined the Gray image of a Z4-linear code and applied CSS construction to the image as well as the binary codes associated to any Z4-linear codes: torsion and residue codes. To explain CSS construction, we recall some relevant basic facts about classical codes. For any u = ( u1, . . . , u n) and v = ( v1, . . . , v n)

in Fnq , the (Euclidean) inner product u · v is defined as 

u · v = u1v1 + u2v2 + · · · + unvn.

The dual code of C is defined as 

C⊥ = {v ∈ Fnq : u · v = 0 for all u ∈ C}.

A code C that is contained in its dual, C ⊆ C⊥, is called self-orthogonal or weakly self-dual. Acode C that contains its dual, C⊥ ⊆ C, is called dual-containing. If C = C⊥ then we say that C is self-dual. If C ∩ C⊥ = {0} then C (and C⊥) is called a linear complementary dual (LCD) code.

k ≤ n − d + 1 . Codes whose parameters attain the equality in this bound are called MDS codes which are optimal. The quantum version of the Singleton bound is k ≤ n − 2d + 1 [12, Theorem 23]. A quantum code whose parameters attain the equality in this bound is called a quantum MDS code.
