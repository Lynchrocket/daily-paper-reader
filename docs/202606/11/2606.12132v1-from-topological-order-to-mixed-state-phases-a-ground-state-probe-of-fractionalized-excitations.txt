## From Topological Order to Mixed-State Phases: A Ground-State Probe of Fractionalized Excitations 

Yunlong Zang, 1, ∗ Yu-Bin Li, 1, ∗ and Shenghan Jiang 1, †

> 1

Kavli Institute for Theoretical Sciences and School of Quantum, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China 

(Dated: June 11, 2026) How do we detect topological phases from a single ground state? Entanglement entropy and spectrum have long been the standard tools — but the reduced density matrix (RDM) itself contains far more information. We show that the RDM of a 2D topologically ordered system, expressed at the entanglement cut, realizes a 1D mixed-state phase. For the Z2 toric code phase, it is a 1D Z2

strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SW-SSB) phase, where deconfinement of anyons manifests as the short-range correlation of both Z2 charge and Z2 domain-wall in the RDM. The bulk 

e-m duality translates into a Kramers–Wannier self-duality of the SW-SSB phase. Extending the framework to gapped Z2 spin liquids, the global spin-rotation symmetry manifests as an additional weak symmetry for the 1D RDM. Spin- 12 spinons result in a cusp on the disorder parameter of spin-rotation at θ = π, providing a direct, ground-state signature of symmetry fractionalization. We verify this prediction analytically using the matrix product density operator formalism and numerically for the kagome-lattice resonating valence bond state. The proposed observable requires only a single ground-state wavefunction, making it amenable to quantum simulation platforms. 

Introduction. Topologically ordered phases, charac-terized by long-range entanglement and anyonic excita-tions, stand as a cornerstone of modern condensed matter physics [1–4]. While routinely identified by global signa-tures such as ground-state degeneracy on the torus [5], their experimental detection remains a formidable chal-lenge — one that demands new theoretical tools beyond conventional order parameters. Entanglement-based diagnostics have partially filled this gap. The topological entanglement entropy (TEE) — a universal subleading constant in the area law — quantifies the total quantum dimension of anyons [6, 7]. The entanglement spectrum mimics edge-mode disper-sions, particularly for chiral phases [8]. These measures are derived from the reduced density matrix (RDM), sug-gesting that the RDM itself should encode richer infor-mation about the underlying topological order[9, 10]. The key insight of this work is that the RDM of a 2D topological phase, evaluated at the entanglement cut, realizes a nontrivial one-dimensional mixed state phase [11–19]. This pure–mixed correspondence — re-cently explored from complementary perspectives [20–25] — opens a new window into topological order through the lens of mixed-state phases. Specifically, for the Z2

toric code topological order, we demonstrate that its RDM realizes a 1D Z2 strong-to-weak spontaneous sym-metry breaking (SW-SSB) phase [13, 18, 26–32], in which a strong symmetry ( ρ = U ρ = ρU )[11, 33] is sponta-neously broken to a weak one ( ρ = U ρU †). Here, the strong Z2 symmetry inherited from the bulk one-form symmetry [34, 35] is spontaneously broken to a weak one, and this SW-SSB directly signals the deconfinement of anyons. This correspondence is powerful in two complemen-tary ways. First, it inspires a novel definition of 1D SW-SSB phases based solely on linear correlators : the charged-operator correlator and the domain wall cor-relator [36, 37] both decay exponentially, manifesting the deconfinement of e and m anyons. The bulk e-

m electromagnetic duality translates into the Kramers-Wannier self-duality of 1D SW-SSB phase[38, 39]. Sec-ond, the framework, when extended to gapped Z2 spin liquids [13, 40–43], leads to a powerful new method for detecting symmetry fractionalization [44, 45]. We prove analytically — using a matrix product density operator (MPDO) description of the 1D RDM — that the spin-rotation disorder parameter ⟨UA(θ)⟩ [46–55] develops a 

non-analytic cusp at rotation angle θ = π when e-anyon carries spin- 12 . We verify this prediction numerically us-ing the nearest-neighbor resonating valence bond (NN-RVB) state on the kagome lattice. This provides a di-rect, ground-state probe of spinons that requires only a single wavefunction, which is readily implementable on quantum simulation platforms.

The 1D SW-SSB phase. In summary, the 1D RDM 

ρ∂A of a generic Z2 topological ordered state exhibits the following universal properties: 1. Z2 strong symmetry, indicating zero e;2. PBC, signaling zero m;3. Exponential decay of both Z2 charge and Z2

domain-wall correlators (Eq. (10)), signaling anyon deconfinement. Notably, property 3 cannot occur in any pure ground state(s) of a Z2 symmetric Ising chains[37]. We define the 1D strong Z2 symmetric ρ∂A whose cor-relation function satisfies Eq. (10) as the strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SW-SSB) mixed-state phase. Physically, the exponential decay of Z2 domain wall operators signals that the strong Z2 symmetry is broken. Meanwhile, operator τ z carries charge under the induced Z2 weak symmetry (the diagonal subgroup of 

Z2 ×Z2); hence, the exponential decay of ⟨τ zi τ zj ⟩ indicates that the Z2 weak symmetry remains unbroken. Our def-inition of SW-SSB — based solely on linear observables in ρ∂A — is distinct from characterizations via fidelity correlators [18] or Wightman functions [28, 29] involving 

√ρ. Such linear observables are directly accessible in ex-periments and numerical simulations, whereas extracting 

√ρ in general requires full state tomography. A detailed comparison between these approaches is provided in sup-plemental section II. The pure–mixed correspondence extends to all three phases of Z2 gauge theory [58], completing the dictionary: 

• Deconfined phase ←→ SW-SSB phase : both the charged-operator and domain-wall correlators decay exponentially (Eq. (10)). 

• Confined phase (m condenses) ←→ symmetric phase : domain walls proliferate while the charged correlator decays. 

• Higgs phase (e condenses) ←→ SSB phase : the 

Z2 charge condenses and long-range ordered while the domain wall decays; the strong symmetry is fully broken. This three-way correspondence was first proposed in the PEPS context by Ref. [59], where the 1D boundary MPS fixed points of the deformed toric code PEPS were dis-cussed. We provide a detailed derivation and numerical verification in supplemental sections I and IV. 

Application: Detecting Symmetry Fractionalization 

The pure-mixed correspondence between 2D topological phases and 1D mixed-state phases is more than a formal mapping – it provides a practical framework for extract-ing universal observables directly from a single ground state wavefunction. Here, we generalize this framework to gapped Z2 spin liquids with global spin-rotation sym-metry, where the e-anyon carries spin- 12 , identified as the fractionalized spinon. The crucial new ingredient is the global spin-rotation symmetry, which manifests as a weak 

symmetry of the 1D mixed state ρ∂A — in contrast to the Z2 strong symmetry that encodes the topological or-der. A classic example is the NN-RVB state [40] on a frustrated spin- 12 system [40]. While spinons are typically probed through the dy-namic spin structure factor [60] – where they produce a low-energy continuum instead of sharp spin-wave peaks – this spectral feature is often obscured by impurities experimentally [43, 61]. Summary and discussion. The central message of this work is that the RDM of a 2D topological phase, eval-uated at an entanglement cut, realizes a 1D nontriv-ial mixed-state phase, whose symmetry and correlation structure directly encodes universal topological data. For the Z2 toric code, the RDM at the entanglement cut re-alizes a 1D Z2 SW-SSB phase, whose self-duality under the Kramers–Wannier transformation reflects the elec-tromagnetic duality of the bulk topological order. By ex-tending this framework to the gapped Z2 spin liquids, we propose a direct ground-state diagnostic of spin-rotation symmetry fractionalization, where the corresponding dis-order parameter develops non-analytic cusp at θ = π.Analytical derivations based on MPDO and numerical results for the kagome-lattice RVB state are provided to corroborate this prediction. Looking forward, our work suggests several promis-ing directions. The linear correlators definition of SW-SSB phase readily generalizes to d-dimensional mixed states with a strong n-form symmetry[62]. In this case, both the order parameter ⟨Oc⟩ (supported on a closed 

n-dimensional manifold Mc) and the disorder parameter 

⟨Od⟩ (supported on a ( d − n)-dimensional manifold with boundary Md) obey “volume-law” scaling: 

− ln |⟨ Oc⟩| ∝ V cn+1 , − ln |⟨ Od⟩| ∝ V dd−n , (29) 6where V cn+1 is the minimal volume of manifolds bounded by Mc, and V dd−n is the volume of Md.The natural extension of the pure–mixed correspon-dence to topological phases with non-invertible (categor-ical) symmetries [35, 63] is particularly compelling, as the framework may shed new light on detection of such exotic orders. Extending these ideas to chiral topological phases remains an important open problem. The most immediate implication of this work is exper-imental: the disorder parameter ⟨UA(θ)⟩ is a concrete, measurable observable that can be accessed on quantum simulation platforms. It requires only a single ground-state wavefunction, making it a practical route to detect-ing signatures of fractionalized excitations in engineered quantum systems.

> I. RDM FOR THE TORIC CODE PHASE BEYOND THE FIXED POINT

In the main text, we established the pure–mixed correspondence at the exactly solvable (fixed-point) limit of the toric code. Second, we derive the same correspondence from the projected-entangled-pair state (PEPS) framework, where the entanglement space is identified with the virtual legs at the entanglement cut. 10
