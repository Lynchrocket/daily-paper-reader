# Spectral and Additive Combinatorial Methods for Cycles and Absorbing Sets in Lifted-Product Quantum LDPC Codes 

## Aida Abiad ∗ Nichola Castriota †

Abstract 

The finite-length performance of quantum low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes under iterative decoding is governed by small substructures of the Tanner graph, principally short cycles and absorbing sets. While the classical theory of these sub-structures for quasi-cyclic codes is well developed through discrete Fourier transform (DFT) methods, these tools do not directly address the two-block tensor structure 

HX = [ eH1 | I ⊗ eBT ] of the lifted-product (quasi-cyclic generalised hypergraph product, QC-GHP) codes that dominate current quantum LDPC constructions. In this paper we develop a quantum-specific spectral framework that exploits this structure. At its core is a DFT block-diagonalisation of HX HTX that reduces moment-trace and cycle computations from an ( r1ℓ) × (r1ℓ) matrix to a sum of ℓ

small r1 × r1 Hermitian matrices, with the second block entering only as a scalar shift. From this result we derive a closed-form 4-cycle count for generalised bicycle codes via additive energies, a joint Sidon characterisation of girth 6 in the spirit of Fossorier’s classical criterion, a Fourier expression for the number of (3 , 3) elemen-tary absorbing sets in column-weight-3 codes via the Wang–Dolecek–Wesel triangle bijection, and a lower bound on stopping-set sizes using the expander mixing lemma. 

Keywords: quantum LDPC codes, CSS codes, lifted product codes, generalised bi-cycle codes, Tanner graph, absorbing sets, stopping sets, discrete Fourier transform, additive energy, Sidon sets, expander mixing lemma

Low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes , introduced by Gallager [20] for the classical setting and later studied in the quantum setting, beginning with the sparse-graph constructions of MacKay, Mitchison, and McFadden [32] and notably through the generalised-bicycle construction of Kovalev and Pryadko [29], are among the leading candi-date families for both regimes because they admit fast iterative decoders whose complexity grows only linearly with the block length. In practice, at the realistic block lengths used in hardware, the iterative decoder of an LDPC code does not meet the asymptotic guarantees promised by coding theory: its failure probability eventually flattens out as the noise decreases, and the configurations responsible for this error floor are small recurring patterns in the bipartite Tanner graph 

associated to the code’s parity-check matrix [41, 15]. Two such patterns have repeatedly been singled out. Short cycles disrupt the independence assumptions on which iterative decoding rests and cause the decoder to oscillate, and absorbing sets are small vertex con-figurations where erroneous messages reinforce one another and become locally stable; the latter have been recognised as the dominant failure mechanism of iterative decoders across a wide range of channels [15, 33]. Morris, Pllaha, and Kelley [34] have recently shown that absorbing sets also drive decoder failure in Calderbank–Shor–Steane (CSS) quantum LDPC codes, so the same two graphical patterns govern the finite-length performance of classical and quantum LDPC codes alike. Controlling these patterns turns the engineering question of code design into a com-binatorial problem: how many few short cycles can a sparse Tanner graph have, and how small can its absorbing sets be? On the classical side, a considerable amount of work tackles this question through the spectrum of the parity-check matrix. For quasi-cyclic (QC) LDPC codes built from a cyclic lift, the discrete Fourier transform (DFT) block-diagonalises the relevant matrices and reduces cycle counts to small eigenvalue com-putations: this is the eigenvalue reduction of Smarandache and Flanagan [43] and the directed-edge-matrix recipe of Karimi and Banihashemi [27], while Fossorier [19] charac-terised the girth of QC codes built from circulant permutation matrices through exponent-difference conditions and Wang, Dolecek, and Wesel [48] gave a bijection between the smallest absorbing sets of column-weight-3 codes and triangles in an associated graph. On the quantum side, by contrast, cycle and absorbing-set counts of concrete code fam-ilies have so far been obtained largely by direct enumeration [38] rather than through structural spectral tools, and the absorbing-set theory of CSS codes is only beginning to be developed [34]. A main obstacle is that the leading quantum LDPC constructions, the lifted-product (and quasi-cyclic generalised hypergraph product, QC-GHP) codes of Panteleev and Kalachev [38], have an X-check matrix of the two-block tensor form 

HX = [ eH1 | I ⊗ eBT ], which the single-block classical DFT machinery does not directly address. In this work we address this gap by developing a quantum-specific spectral framework that exploits the two-block tensor form rather than working around it (Theorem 4.1). The 2main tool is a DFT block-diagonalisation of HX HTX that captures the contribution of the second block as a scalar shift, reducing moment-trace and cycle computations on an ( r1ℓ)×

(r1ℓ) ambient matrix to a sum of ℓ small r1 × r1 Hermitian matrices. As a consequence of the proposed spectral framework, we obtain, for generalised bicycle (GB) codes, a closed-form count of 4-cycles in terms of the additive energies of the support sets of the defining polynomials (Proposition 4.9) together with a joint Sidon-type characterisation of girth 6 (Corollary 4.11) that parallels, in the single-circulant setting, Fossorier’s classical exponent-difference criterion [19]; through the triangle bijection of Wang, Dolecek, and Wesel [48], we derive a Fourier expression for the number of (3 , 3) elementary absorbing sets in column-weight-3 QC-GHP codes (Proposition 4.17), addressing an enumeration question raised by the absorbing-set framework of Morris, Pllaha, and Kelley [34]; and, applying the bipartite expander mixing lemma through the second-largest singular value of HX obtained from the same Fourier matrices, we show a spectral lower bound on the stopping distance of a QC-GHP code (Proposition 5.1). All of these expressions are computable in closed form from the polynomial data of the lifted-product construction, and together they specialise and unify the classical single-block spectral methods of [43, 27, 19, 48] to the quantum two-block setting.

2.2.1 Critical substructures: stopping, trapping, and absorbing sets 

Throughout this subsection, G = ( V, W ; E) is the Tanner graph of a binary linear code

A stopping set S in a Tanner graph G = ( V, W ; E)is a subset S ⊆ V of variable nodes such that every check node in the neighborhood N (S)is connected to S by at least two edges. is a subset of a variable nodes whose induced subgraph GT contains exactly b odd-degree check nodes. Figure 1(b) shows a (2 , 2)-trapping set T = {v1, v 2} with its two odd-degree checks drawn in red. 

Definition 2.6 (Elementary, leafless, and non-elementary trapping sets, [10]) . A trapping set is called elementary (ETS) if every check node in its induced subgraph has degree one or two. An elementary trapping set is leafless (LETS) if, additionally, every variable node is adjacent to at least two degree-two (satisfied) check nodes. An ETS that is not leafless is called an ETS with leaf (ETSL). A trapping set that is not elementary is called a non-elementary trapping set (NETS). A related global construction is the variable-node graph , attached to the Tanner graph itself rather than to one of its substructures. 

Definition 2.7 (Variable-node graph) . The variable-node graph of a Tanner graph G =(V, W ; E), denoted GV N , is the simple graph on the variable set V in which u, v ∈ V are adjacent iff there exists a check c ∈ W with {u, c }, {v, c } ∈ E. When g(G) ≥ 6, any such pair {u, v } shares at most one common check, so GV N has no multi-edges. Absorbing sets refine trapping sets by adding a local stability condition: every variable node in the set has strictly more satisfied (even-degree) than unsatisfied (odd-degree) check neighbours in the induced subgraph [14, 16]. This local majority is what makes absorbing sets “absorb” the decoder [15].

The set A is an ( a, b )-absorbing set if exactly b check nodes in GA have odd degree and ev > o v for every v ∈ A . ## 2.3 Quantum LDPC codes 

Quantum error-correcting codes encode k logical qubits into n physical qubits, embedding the logical state into a structured subspace of ( C2)⊗n; see Breuckmann and Eberhardt [5] for a survey. We work with the stabilizer codes of Gottesman [22] and Calderbank-Rains-Shor-Sloane [7], and specifically with the Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) subclass, whose stabilizer factors into pure-X and pure-Z generators. In the n-qubit Pauli group any two elements commute or anticommute, and commutation is governed by the symplectic inner product on Fn 

> 2

[7, 22]; this is the structural reason CSS codes admit a binary-matrix description. We focus on the QLDPC subclass (Definition 2.10), whose running example is the quasi-cyclic generalized hypergraph product (QC-GHP) family of [38].

# 3 Lifted-product QLDPC codes and the absorbing-set framework 

Quantum LDPC (QLDPC) codes are among the leading current candidates for scalable fault-tolerant quantum computation [21, 37, 30]. As in the classical case, their finite-length performance is determined not by asymptotics but by a small number of stable graphical configurations in the Tanner graph that cause persistent decoder failure [41, 16], and these critical substructures are far less understood in the quantum setting [8, 45, 34, 2] than in the classical one [10, 33, 3, 35, 36]. A systematic spectral graph-theoretic ap-proach to QLDPC Tanner graphs, parallel to the adjacency-spectrum cycle counts used extensively for classical LDPC codes [11, 27, 4], has not yet appeared: the CSS com-mutation constraint and the lifted-product block structure pose additional obstructions. This work takes a first step via a discrete Fourier transform (DFT)-based cycle-counting framework for lifted-product Tanner graphs. 

## 3.1 QLDPC codes and the absorbing-set framework 

Both structural threads to date concern syndrome-based iterative decoding (§2.3.3), the natural decoding model for stabilizer codes [39]. Raveendran and Vasi´ c [40] identify 

symmetric stabilizers , a genuinely quantum failure mode in which decoder degeneracy lets belief propagation oscillate between equivalent error estimates rather than converge. Morris, Pllaha, and Kelley [34] show that absorbing sets (Definition 2.8) are the dominant failure mechanism for syndrome-based iterative decoders: any ( a, b )-absorbing set with 

b ≥ 1 is failure-inducing (its syndrome estimate stays 0, permanently mismatching the input syndrome at the b odd-degree checks), while the b = 0 case subsumes the symmetric-stabilizer phenomenon and governs the degenerate-versus-logical-error dichotomy [34]. For CSS hypergraph-product codes [47] the absorbing sets are completely characterised by the 9base matrices H1, H2 and their transposes, so small base-code absorbing sets propagate to low-weight failure-inducing sets [34]. Absorbing sets thus give a single combinatorial object subsuming the symmetric-stabilizer formalism of [40], which is why subsequent QLDPC structural analyses [45, 8, 34] have converged on them. Moreover, the classical Hoory-type cage and girth lower bounds on absorbing-set sizes [25, 33] do not transfer to the QLDPC two-block form 

HX = [ eH1 | Ir1 ⊗ eBT ], because the CSS commutation constraint HX HTZ = 0 forces girth at most 6 in any fully connected quasi-cyclic construction with column weight at least 3 [1], foreclosing the high-girth regime in which Hoory-type counting gives strong bounds.

# 4 A spectral framework for cycle counting for lifted-product Tanner graphs 

In this section we adapt and extend the classical spectral cycle-counting framework of Smarandache and Flanagan [43] and Karimi and Banihashemi [27] to the Tanner graphs of lifted-product QLDPC codes [38]. For cyclic lifts over G = Zℓ, the biadjacency matrix of the Tanner graph block-diagonalises under the discrete Fourier transform, reducing cycle-counting traces to a sum over ℓ small matrices [43, Thm. 1, Cor. 2], [27, Theorem 2]; in our setting this enters through Theorem 4.1 below. For classical QC-LDPC codes the reduction is due to Smarandache and Flanagan [43], who showed that the eigenvalues of the rL × rL matrix HT H for an L-fold cyclic lift of a J × r protograph reduce to those of 

L matrices of size r × r obtained by Fourier evaluation; Karimi and Banihashemi [27] give an alternative recipe via the directed edge matrix; and Smarandache and Mitchell [44] derive explicit closed forms for N2k in any biregular Tanner graph, valid for k ≤ 7. On the quantum side, Zhang [50] carries out a finite-length combinatorial cycle analy-sis of the lifted product construction of [38] (and its left-right generalisation), focusing on dominant 8-cycles and proving a girth upper bound showing that 8-cycles are unavoid-able regardless of the group-algebra elements, unlike the classical QC-LDPC case. Our framework is complementary: where Zhang enumerates 8-cycle topologies case by case, we develop a Fourier-spectral identity (Theorem 4.1) that translates short-cycle counts into traces of small matrices, applies uniformly to all even cycle lengths 2 k up to the girth bound, and yields the additive-energy/Sidon-type characterisations of §4 absent from [50]. This section specialises the classical single-block framework to the quantum two-block structure HX = [ eH1 | Ir1 ⊗ eBT ] (Theorem 4.1, where the second block con-tributes only a scalar shift |b(ωj )|2Ir1 to each block-diagonal matrix; see Remark 4.2), gives an additive-combinatorial reading of the GB-code 4-cycle formula via additive ener-13 gies (Lemma 4.6, Proposition 4.9), and a joint Sidon condition characterising girth-6 GB codes (Corollary 4.11).

We have developed a quantum-specific spectral framework for the lifted-product (QC-GHP) and generalised bicycle Tanner graphs of the leading quantum LDPC construc-tions. The central tool is the DFT block-diagonalisation of HX HTX (Theorem 4.1), which exploits the two-block tensor form HX = [ eH1 | Ir1 ⊗ eBT ] by absorbing the second block into a scalar shift |b(ωj )|2Ir1 and reduces an ( r1ℓ) × (r1ℓ) moment-trace computation to 

ℓ traces of r1 × r1 Hermitian matrices, extending the classical single-block reductions of Smarandache–Flanagan [43] and Karimi–Banihashemi [27] to the quantum setting. To illustrate applications of the proposed spectral method, we obtain, for generalised bicy-cle codes, a 4-cycle count through additive energies (Proposition 4.9) and a joint Sidon characterisation of girth 6 echoing Fossorier’s criterion [19] (Corollary 4.11); via the Wang– Dolecek–Wesel triangle bijection [48], a closed-form Fourier count of the (3 , 3) elementary absorbing sets of column-weight-3 QC-GHP codes (Proposition 4.17), addressing a ques-tion raised by the framework of Morris, Pllaha, and Kelley [34]; and, through the bipartite expander mixing lemma applied to the second-largest singular value computed from the same Fourier matrices, a spectral-gap lower bound on stopping-set sizes (Proposition 5.1). We conclude with a few open problems. 

• Higher even-cycle and absorbing-set counts. Extending the closed-form counts be-yond 4- and 6-cycles requires the additive 3-energy ( T3) computations and the girth-8 criterion noted in Remark 4.13, together with a length-2 j analogue of the (3 , 3)-to-triangle bijection in order to translate higher cycle counts into absorbing-set counts. 

• Non-vacuous stopping-set bounds. For the column-weight-3 examples studied here the spectral-gap bound is vacuous, since the second singular value exceeds pγ/ 2. Identifying QC-GHP design parameters, for instance larger column weight or Ramanujan-type lifted-product constructions, under which the bound becomes in-formative is left for future work, as is a Tanner-style multi-eigenvalue tightening of the expander mixing step. 

• Other elementary absorbing-set types. The (4 , 2) and (4 , 0) absorbing-set cases are accessible in principle through the same spectral data, via induced “ K4 minus an edge” and induced K4 counts in the variable-node graph, but they require the ad-ditional subgraph-elimination corrections sketched in Remark 4.13.
