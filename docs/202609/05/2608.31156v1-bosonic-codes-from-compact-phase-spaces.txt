(Dated: September 1, 2026) We present the algebraic structure of bosonic quantum error-correcting codes on genus-two Rie-mann surfaces. We explicitly construct the code words as automorphic forms and analytically generate the full tower of code spaces at all weights. We prove a fundamental no-go theorem: for any genus greater than one, the stabilizer group is non-amenable, forcing a strictly positive spectral gap in the stabilizer Hamiltonian. Consequently, no normalizable quantum state can satisfy all sta-bilizer conditions. This sharply contrasts with standard Gottesman–Kitaev–Preskill (GKP) codes, where the amenability of the stabilizer group Z2 permits approximate code words with arbitrary precision. 

Introduction . The Gottesman–Kitaev–Preskill (GKP) code protects quantum information in a single bosonic mode by imposing a lattice of displacement symmetries on phase space [1]. The quotient of the phase plane by this lattice is a torus (an elliptic curve), and the code words are theta functions on it, holomorphic sections of a line bundle arising from geometric quantization, a per-spective developed in [2–4]. Crucially, the torus is the 

phase space of the oscillator, not a configuration space: the elliptic curve emerges physically as the image of the coherent-state family projected into the code space [5]. Because the phase space is compact, the code space is finite-dimensional, with its dimension governed by the Riemann–Roch theorem. This raises a sharp question: what bosonic codes arise when the phase space is a com-pact surface of genus g ≥ 2? Here we address this question for compact hyperbolic Riemann surfaces, with genus two as the first nontriv-ial case. Such a surface is the hyperbolic disk D folded up by a discrete group Γ. The symmetries of the disk form the group PSU(1 , 1), whose unitary action on two bosonic modes is by the quadratic Gaussian transforma-tions of SU(1 , 1). Taking Γ as the stabilizer group there-fore makes the stabilizers squeezing transformations, the hyperbolic counterpart of the displacements of GKP. The code words are automorphic forms on D, their number fixed by the Riemann–Roch theorem. We report two complementary results. Both the sta-bilizers and the logical gates of these two-mode codes are Gaussian operations (squeezing and rotations of two bosonic modes), yet their logical gate group is the under-lying surface’s automorphism group, and by Greenberg’s theorem [6] every finite group arises this way. Framework. —We take as our starting point a general quantum phase space: a coherent-state family holomor-phically parametrized by a complex manifold C, i.e., a holomorphic map C → P(H) into the space of rays in H [8, 9]. On the complex plane, the Bargmann repre-sentation z 7 → | z⟩ provides such a family globally: each point z ∈ C indexes a coherent state, and the depen-dence on z is holomorphic. Given a state ψ, we define its 

stellar function f ψ (z) = ⟨ψ|z⟩ (antilinear in ψ, with no normalization factor), adapting conventions of Ref. [10] for reasons that will be clear shortly. On the plane, f ψ

is entire, so its zeros, which encode the non-Gaussian structure of ψ, can be infinite in number and carry no topological constraint. Now suppose the phase space is a compact Riemann surface C of genus g; that is, suppose we have a coherent-state family parametrized by C [9]. The map C → P(H)is well defined globally, but every holomorphic function on C is constant, so it admits no global holomorphic lift to H [5]. Holomorphic lifts exist only locally: one works with frames z 7 → | z⟩α on coordinate patches Uα,related on overlaps by nowhere-vanishing holomorphic functions |z⟩α = gαβ (z) |z⟩β . These transition functions define a holomorphic line bundle L → C; the stellar functions f ψα (z) = ⟨ψ|z⟩α on each patch together con-stitute a global holomorphic section of the dual bun-dle L := L∗. Unlike the planar case, where f ψ is a single entire function, the compact geometry forces f ψ to be defined only locally, with chart-to-chart transformations dictated by L.Compactness makes the stellar representation rigid :the stellar rank of every state is the same topological in-variant, deg L. Two states with the same zero locus are proportional [11]: every state is pinned, up to a scalar, by a finite constellation of points on C. This generalizes the Majorana constellation on the sphere ( g = 0) [12, 13] and the stellar hierarchy on the plane [10]. The stellar transform ψ 7 → f ψ identifies the Hilbert space with the space of global sections Γ( C, L) of the line bundle L; for stellar rank r = deg L > 2g−2, the Riemann–Roch theo-rem gives dim H = r − g + 1 . (1) Furthermore, r := deg L = A/ 2π [14], where A is the area of C; hence the formula reads as a generalization of the Bohr-Sommerfeld rule to phase spaces with nontrivial topology.

Corollary 2 (Amenability dichotomy) . Among the bosonic stabilizer codes on compact Riemann surfaces of genus g ≥ 1 constructed here—plane and discrete-series representations—approximate code words of arbitrarily high precision exist if and only if g = 1 .Discussion . This work completes a trinity of continuous-variable quantum codes from compact, two-dimensional phase spaces, classified by curvature and genus g. On the sphere ( g = 0), the stabilizer group is finite and exact code words exist; examples include spin [29] and the spherical-Landau-level [30] codes. On the flat torus ( g = 1), the stabilizer group Z2 is infinite but amenable, and the GKP codes [1] admit approximate code words of arbitrary precision at the cost of increasing energy. We settle the hyperbolic case g ≥ 2: the non-amenability of the surface group opens a spectral gap (Theorem 1) that forbids even approximate stabilizer eigenstates. Amenability of π1(Σ g ) is the sharp divid-ing line (Corollary 2). The infinite-dimensional setting is essential: in finite dimension every generalized eigenvec-tor is normalizable, so a nonzero generalized code space cannot coexist with a positive bound on all normaliz-able states. The closest finite-dimensional analogue is the no-low-energy-trivial-states (NLTS) theorem [31, 32]: for certain families of qubit stabilizer Hamiltonians whose in-teraction graphs are expander graphs, every state below a fixed energy density is nontrivial, i.e. unreachable by a constant-depth circuit. So expansion is a kind of finite-family analogue of non-amenability [5], but it removes 6only the trivial low-energy states; here non-amenability removes them all. The obstruction, however, is intrinsically a state-ment about the exact unitary stabilizers ˆDγ . Finite-energy analogues—in which these unitaries are deformed by a non-unitary envelope to confine the system’s en-ergy—need not inherit this gap. For standard GKP codes, precisely this strategy underlies the Trotterized stabilization protocols of Royer et al. [33], which engi-neer dissipation toward the +1 eigenspace of finite-energy stabilizers. Determining if these dissipative stabilization schemes can be adapted to tame the non-amenable ge-ometry of higher-genus curves is an interesting open chal-lenge for realizing these codes in hardware.

C. No global holomorphic lift on compact surfaces 

We prove the claim in the main text that a holomorphic coherent-state family parametrized by a compact Riemann surface C admits no global holomorphic section. 

Proof. Suppose z 7 → | z⟩ were a global holomorphic map from C to H (not just to P(H)). For any |ϕ⟩ ∈ H , the function 

g(z) = ⟨ϕ|z⟩ would be holomorphic on the compact surface C, hence constant by the maximum principle. Since this holds for every |ϕ⟩, the map z 7 → | z⟩ itself is constant—contradicting the assumption that it parametrizes a non-trivial coherent-state family. More precisely, the obstruction is topological. The coherent-state map Ψ : C → P(H) defines the holomorphic line bundle L = Ψ ∗O(−1), which has negative degree for any non-constant holomorphic map to projective space. A global holomorphic lift would provide a nowhere-vanishing section of L, impossible for a line bundle of nonzero degree. > SIV. CODE WORDS: ELLIPTIC-CURVE EQUATIONS AND THE CANONICAL RING

The Kodaira map (S11) realizes the compact phase space as a projective algebraic variety inside the projectivized code space, so the code words satisfy polynomial equations—and every such equation is a polynomial identity among coherent-state overlaps. This section makes those equations explicit at genus one and at genus two. At genus one they are the classical equations of elliptic curves, dictated by the representation theory of the theta group of Sec. SIII A, following Mumford [5]. At genus two the same algebraicity is governed by the canonical ring, which we compute in closed form; its first output is the conic Y 2 = XZ satisfied by the k = 2 code words, and its last the equation of the hyperelliptic curve itself, recovered as an identity among code words. A closing subsection supplies the analytic construction of the code words on every phase space uniformized by the hyperbolic disk: Poincar´ e theta series, hyperbolic analogues of the Jacobi theta functions.

> C. Hyperbolic theta series

> SVIII. BEYOND INTEGER WEIGHT: SPIN STRUCTURES AND A NON-SPLIT GATE GROUP

At half-integral Bargmann index, the logical extension need not split. Two distinct obstructions occur on the Bolza surface. For the odd theta characteristics, the obstruction is purely a double-cover obstruction: no choice of signs realizes the classical group inside SU (1 , 1), although arbitrary scalar rephasing linearizes the resulting projective action.
