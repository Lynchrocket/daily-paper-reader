ENTANGLEMENT ASSISTED QUANTUM (r, δ )-LOCALLY RECOVERABLE CODES 

C. GALINDO, F. HERNANDO, H. MARTÍN-CRUZ AND R. MATSUMOTO 

Abstract. Quantum ( r, δ )-locally recoverable codes are quantum error-correcting codes capable of correcting δ − 1 qudit erasures within one subset of qudits of cardinality at most r + δ − 1. In this paper, we introduce the more general framework of entanglement-assisted quan-tum ( r, δ )-locally recoverable codes, assuming that the local recovery operation is assisted by receiver-held qudits that remain unaffected by erasures. We establish necessary and sufficient conditions for these codes to satisfy this property. For codes derived from Hermitian or Euclidean constructions, we establish connections between entanglement-assisted quantum and classical notions of ( r, δ )-local recoverability, and derive a Singleton-like bound. Furthermore, we construct optimal pure entangle-ment-assisted quantum ( r, δ )-locally recoverable codes from several fam-ilies of classical codes, including bivariate J-affine variety codes, BCH codes, and homothetic-BCH codes. 

1. Introduction 

Recent evidence of quantum supremacy [3, 5, 65, 46] highlights the po-tential of quantum information processing across numerous practical appli-cations. Protecting quantum states from noise and decoherence is essential to ensure the reliability of quantum devices. Although the no-cloning theo-rem dictates that quantum information cannot be cloned [19, 63], quantum error correction remains viable [58, 60], and quantum error-correcting codes 

(QECCs) are designed for this purpose. The initial work on QECCs was restricted to the binary case [12, 33, 13, 11], but the theory was later extended to non-binary systems. Since then, a wide variety of algebraic methods have been developed to construct non-binary quantum codes (see, for instance, [8, 4, 36, 1, 35, 39, 22, 2]). (Quantum) stabilizer codes constitute an important class of QECCs be-cause they are associated with classical additive codes. Specifically, an (( n, K, d )) q stabilizer code exists if and only if there is an additive clas-sical code C ⊆ F2nq that is self-orthogonal under the trace-symplectic form, where Fq denotes the q-ary finite field (see Theorem 2.2 for more details). Algebraic constructions can be derived using Hermitian or Euclidean inner products over Fnq2 or Fnq , respectively. The restrictive self-orthogonality condition mentioned above (or dual-containment when considering the corresponding dual code) prevents many standard classical codes from being converted into quantum stabilizer codes. To overcome this limitation, Brun et al. [9] introduced the framework of entanglement-assisted quantum error correction (EAQEC) by pre-sharing maximally entangled states between the sender and receiver. This approach simplifies quantum error-correcting theory and enhances transmission capac-ity. Moreover, it enables the construction of entanglement-assisted QECCs (EAQECCs) from arbitrary linear codes via symplectic forms, or Hermitian or Euclidean inner products. The parameters of an EAQECC are denoted [[ n, k, d ; c]] q, where c represents the number of required maximally entangled pairs. Formulas for these parameters in the non-binary setting are given in [28] and recalled in Theorem 2.5. Many major technology companies store data in large-scale cloud storage and distributed computing systems, where server and node failures are rou-tine challenges. To efficiently restore lost data without accessing the entire system, classical r-locally recoverable codes (r-LRCs or LRCs with locality r)were introduced by Gopalan et al. [32]. These codes enable the recovery of a single erasure by accessing at most r other symbol positions. Recent studies on r-LRCs include [62, 6, 44, 51, 49, 34, 45, 56, 16]. Subsequently, Prakash et al. [54] introduced ( r, δ )-locally recoverable codes (( r, δ )-LRCs or LRCs with locality (r, δ )) to handle more realistic scenarios involving multiple simulta-neous node failures. These codes can repair up to δ − 1 erasures by accessing at most r+δ−1 positions, including the erased ones. Recent works in this di-rection include [59, 18, 40, 49, 61, 34, 17, 64, 20, 55, 37, 10, 23]. Both r-LRCs and ( r, δ )-LRCs obey Singleton-like bounds on their parameters and locality (see Equation (2.1) for the ( r, δ )-LRC case). Codes attaining these bounds are termed optimal , and many of the aforementioned references construct families of optimal LRCs. Driven by rapid advancements in quantum computing and the growing scale of quantum information processing, quantum LRCs have emerged as the natural quantum counterpart to classical LRCs. Furthermore, substan-tial industry investment in quantum technology makes quantum data storage an increasingly viable future paradigm. was taken by Golowich and Guruswami [30, 31], who introduced quantum r-locally recoverable codes (quantum r-LRCs ) to address single-qudit erasures. Subsequent developments on these codes include [57, 47, 42, 41]. The quantum analogue of classical local recovery under multiple erasures was introduced in [26] and is recalled in Section 2.3. Conceptually, a QECC 

Q of length n is ( I, K )-locally recoverable if the action of the completely depolarizing channel on the qudits in I ⊊ K can be reversed by inspecting the remaining qudits in K. A code Q is then defined as a quantum ( r, δ )-LRC if every index i ∈ { 1, . . . , n } belongs to a set K of cardinality at most r + δ − 1 such that Q is ( I, K )-locally recoverable for every subset 

I ⊂ K of cardinality δ − 1. Furthermore, the authors of [26] provide a necessary and sufficient condition for a quantum stabilizer code to be ( r, δ )-locally recoverable. This condition depends solely on the puncturing and shortening at suitable coordinate sets of both the underlying symplectic self-orthogonal code and its symplectic dual. Moreover, when using Hermitian or Euclidean inner products, they establish an equivalence between the classical and quantum notions of ( r, δ )-local recoverability under a mild additional condition. Various parameter bounds for quantum LRCs have been established. The first bound for quantum r-LRCs was derived by Golowich and Guruswami [30, Theorem 36] (note that this bound does not appear in the conference version), with subsequent refinements provided in [47, 42, 41], though some of these apply exclusively to pure codes. A Singleton-like bound for pure quantum ( r, δ )-LRCs derived from Hermitian or Euclidean inner products was introduced in [26]. More recently, [25] presented a family of impure quantum codes that exceed the performance bounds for pure codes in quan-tum local recovery. A formal framework unifying entanglement assistance with ( r, δ )-local re-coverability remained unexplored. The special case of recovery with lo-cality r under a single erasure ( δ = 2) was introduced only very recently, and was restricted to CSS codes [43]. In this paper, we bridge this gap for ( r, δ )-local recoverability by introducing entanglement-assisted quantum 

(r, δ )-locally recoverable codes (EA (r, δ )-QLRCs ); see Definition 3.2. This framework relies on the notion of EA quantum (I, K )-local recoverability 

(Definition 3.1) and generalizes the definition of standard quantum ( r, δ )-LRCs given in [26]. Notably, the quantum operation for local recovery is assisted by c qudits held by the receiver, which are assumed to be unaffected by erasures (see Definition 3.1 and Remark 3.3).
