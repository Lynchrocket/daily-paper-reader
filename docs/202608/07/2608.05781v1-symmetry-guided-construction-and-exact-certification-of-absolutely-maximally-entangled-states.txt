(Dated: August 5, 2026) We construct Hermitian self-dual maximum-distance-separable codes with parameters [12 , 6, 7] 25 ,

[18 , 9, 10] 121 , and [18 , 9, 10] 169 . Through the nonbinary stabilizer construction these codes define

The [12 , 6, 7] 25 code, which is not monomially equivalent to a generalized Reed–Solomon code, is obtained by a systematic search without prescribed coordinate symmetry; its monomial– semilinear automorphism group acts with a regular Z23 orbit on nine coordinates. Imposing that action on two nine-coordinate orbits reduces the length-eighteen search to a nine-element group-algebra kernel, whose Hermitian character decomposition splits the invariant self-duality constraints into independent blocks with exact family counts; a norm-one coordinate scaling removes a factor 

q + 1 = 14 at q = 13 . Hermitian self-duality and the MDS property are certified by exact field arithmetic and complete square-minor enumeration. Graph-state cut-rank computations, including all single-vertex deletions of the length-eighteen graphs, provide additional exact checks. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

A pure state |ψ⟩ ∈ (Cq )⊗n is t-uniform when every reduction to any t parties is maximally mixed. It is 

absolutely maximally entangled , denoted AME (n, q ), when 

t = ⌊n/ 2⌋[ 1, 2 ]. Equivalently, the state is maximally entangled across every bipartition whose smaller side has at most half of the parties. AME states connect to quantum secret sharing, teleportation, perfect tensors, orthogonal arrays, multiunitary matrices, and quantum error correction[ 2 – 4]. In coding language an AME (n, q )

state is a pure quantum code [[ n, 0, ⌊n/ 2⌋ + 1]] q ; for even 

n it is a zero-dimensional quantum MDS code saturating the quantum Singleton bound[5, 6]. Existence is a parameter-by-parameter problem. Some regions are covered by classical MDS constructions, while other cells require graph states, orthogonal arrays, mul-tiunitary matrices, or genuinely quantum constructions. Nonexistence can follow from coding bounds, weight enu-merators, or shadow inequalities; the seven-qubit case is a complete exclusion[ 7, 8]. The Huber–Wyderka table and Grassl’s quantum-code tables organize much of the known existence information[ 9 , 10 ], and the review of Rajchel-Mieldzioć et al. surveys the construction landscape[ 4]. The finer classification of local-unitary, local-Clifford, or SLOCC equivalence classes within a nonempty cell [ 11 –14 ]is not addressed in this paper. > C. One-party projection

Lemma 3 (Projection from even to odd party number) .

If AME (2 m, q ) exists, then AME (2 m − 1, q ) exists. For a graph-state representative, projecting one vertex onto a computational-basis state deletes that vertex, up to local phase corrections. Proof. Measure a distinguished party v in the computa-tional basis. For any (m − 1) -subset T of the remaining parties, the premeasurement marginal on T ∪ { v} is I/q m.Every outcome therefore has probability 1/q , and the conditional marginal on T is I/q m−1. Since T was arbi-trary, the conditional pure state is AME (2 m − 1, q ). In the phase-polynomial form of Eq. (7) , fixing the variable 

xv removes the vertex and leaves at most linear phases on its neighbors, which are local corrections. A direct rank argument also applies. Let Θ certify 

AME (2 m, p ) and delete vertex v. For any S of size m −

1 among the remaining vertices, the original balanced block with row set S ∪ { v} and column set Sc \ { v}

has m independent rows. Its submatrix obtained by removing the row of v therefore has row rank m − 1,exactly the cut block required by Lemma 2 for the deleted graph. The reduction of Lemma 3 is standard; it underlies the correspondence between even-party AME states and threshold quantum secret sharing[2].   

> III. SEARCH METHOD A. A systematic search at length twelve

The AME (12 , 5) construction was found without pre-scribing a coordinate symmetry. The search worked in systematic form 

G = [ Ik | A], AA T = −Ik, (10) with (q, k ) = (5 , 6) . Candidate rows of A were built depth first. At each node the program enforced the already determined entries of Eq. (5) and rejected any partial matrix as soon as a completed square submatrix became singular. The first row was canonicalized under column permutation and norm-one column scaling, so large fami-lies of monomially equivalent branches were never entered. A transparent reference implementation and a vectorized production implementation were cross-validated on seven smaller or previously known cases, including three nega-tive controls, before the production run. In the archived run the first [12 , 6, 7] 25 solution appeared in roughly ten seconds on a desktop CPU. The search stopped at this so-lution; it is not a classification of all [12 , 6, 7] 25 Hermitian self-dual MDS codes. A stand-alone verifier reads only the final 6 × 6 matrix, checks AA T = −I6, and evaluates all 923 minors from scratch. A separate stabilizer-to-graph calculation then produced the matrix in Appendix A, whose 462 balanced cut blocks were checked independently. 4

> E. Template comparison and corrected census

For comparison, we classified a defined universe of al-ternatives: nontrivial untwisted abelian permutation tem-plates on eighteen coordinates, for every abelian H with

> F. Staged enumeration and exact re-verification

The character-block parameterizations were enumer-ated in a fixed lexicographic order. Each candidate was transformed back to systematic matrix form and passed through progressively more expensive tests: field valid-ity and Hermitian self-duality, full rank, small minors, and then the remaining minors. GPU kernels were used only as a discovery screen. Every reported hit was recon-structed and checked with exact CPU arithmetic, and the final published constructions were rerun through stand-alone verifiers that share neither the search code nor its field implementation. The archived partial-search evidence is summarized in Table II and Fig. 3. > VII. DISCUSSION

The searches leave several questions open. The length-twelve search stopped at its first solution, and the q = 11 

and q = 13 runs cover only the intervals of Table II, so the total number of MDS members of the two invariant families is unknown, as is whether the archived hits are monomially or local-Clifford equivalent. An equivalence and automorphism census of the recorded hits, followed by complete enumeration in the reduced character coor-dinates, are natural next computations; Schur-square di-mensions, projective-frame invariants, weight enumerators of derived codes, and graph-state local-complementation invariants apply to that analysis. Local-unitary, local-Clifford, and SLOCC classification of the five cells remains open. The template census of Sec. III E covers untwisted abelian permutation actions on eighteen coordinates. The smaller parity-viable families at each field can be screened next, followed by nonabelian actions, monomial twists, and mixed orbit geometries. The character-pairing ar-gument of Lemma 4 applies whenever the group algebra splits over Fq2 , and the parity condition of Lemma 5 re-mains applicable when the surviving blocks are not all two-dimensional.

> Appendix B: Corrected abelian-template classification

The classifier used for Table I enumerates every finite abelian group H of order at most eighteen satisfying 

gcd (|H|, q ) = 1 and exp (H) | q2 − 1. For each group it generates all subgroups, all multisets of transitive orbit types H/K whose sizes sum to eighteen, and the cor-responding character multiplicities m(χ). It rejects a template whenever a self-paired character has odd multi-plicity and otherwise evaluates Eq. (17) . Templates are counted with their group and stabilizer multiset; a block profile records the group, its orbit-size multiset, and the sorted dimensions of the self-paired and paired blocks. Table V lists the smallest distinct family sizes; the rank-ings there are by distinct numerical family size, not by the number of templates attaining that size. The archived q = 13 screening script generated sub-groups from at most three elements. That suffices for groups of rank at most three but omits, for example, the full subgroup of Z42 and every orbit type that requires it. The corrected classifier generates spans using up to the full rank of the group. It reproduces the legacy result when invoked in compatibility mode and yields the totals reported in Table I. The correction does not enter the constructions or certificates.
