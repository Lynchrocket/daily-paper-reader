# Constructions of Quantum ( r, δ )-LRCs from cyclic codes 

## Rajendra Prasad Rajpurohit 1 and Maheshanand Bhaintwal 1* 1Department of Mathematics, Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee, Roorkee, 247667, India. *Corresponding author(s). E-mail(s): maheshanand@ma.iitr.ac.in; Contributing authors: rajendrarajpurohit20@gmail.com; 

Abstract 

Classical (r, δ ) locally recoverable codes (LRCs) play a central role in distributed data storage systems as they enable an efficient recovery from erasures by access-ing a small number of surviving symbols. Motivated by their prospective use in future quantum data storage and by recent theoretical progress on quantum locally recoverable codes (qLRCs), we investigate the construction of qLRCs from classical cyclic (r, δ )-LRCs. Our approach identifies cyclic LRCs whose defin-ing sets satisfy a dual-containing condition, allowing them to serve as valid CSS ingredients. We present three explicit families of (r, δ )-qLRCs, two of which are optimal with respect to the quantum Singleton-like bound, whenever the codes are pure, thereby providing optimal examples. Additionally, the codes presented in Constructions 2 and 3 have no bound on their lengths with respect to the field size required to obtain these codes. 

Keywords: Cyclic codes, LRCs, Quantum LRCs 

MSC Classification: 94B05 , 94B15 

# 1 Introduction 

Locally recoverable codes (LRCs) were introduced to address the challenge of efficient recovery in distributed storage systems, in the event of node failures, by enabling recovery of erased symbols through a small number of surviving symbols [1]. A code 1

> arXiv:2606.09522v1 [cs.IT] 8 Jun 2026

symbol is said to have locality r if it can be reconstructed by accessing at most r

other symbols, so that each coordinate belongs to a small repair group, called local set , which supports efficient single-erasure recovery with usually low bandwidth than code dimension. To cope with the situation when multiple simultaneous erasures affect a single local set, the concept of ( r, δ )-locality was introduced [2], in which every coordinate lies in a local set of length at most r + δ − 1 and minimum distance at least 

δ, where δ ≥ 2, so that up to δ − 1 erasures in each local set are correctable. A good amount of literature has been developed on classical LRCs in the past decade. A singleton-type bound for ( r, δ )-LRCs is given in [2]. Several optimal con-structions achieving the Singleton-like bound for LRCs have been proposed, including the important work of Tamo and Barg [3] based on good polynomials and subcodes of Reed–Solomon codes. In recent years, numerous constructions have been pro-posed using cyclic and quasi-cyclic codes [4–7], algebraic geometry codes [8], and matrix-product codes [9]. Further developments include LRCs with availability [10], hierarchical locality [11–13], and unequal locality [14]. Collectively, these results form a rich theory connecting algebraic coding techniques with practical distributed storage requirements. Quantum error-correcting codes (QECCs) form the foundation of reliable quantum computation and quantum communication. They protect quantum information from decoherence, noise, and adversarial disturbances by encoding logical qubits into larger systems using stabilizer or subsystem constructions [15]. Particularly, QECCs, which are constructed via the Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) framework, have formed the foundation of modern quantum coding theory, bridging classical error correction with quantum error correction [16, 17]. A recent focus in quantum coding theory concerns locality properties and is moti-vated by practical architectural constraints in near-term quantum devices. Quantum locally recoverable codes (qLRCs) extend the classical notion of LRCs to quantum settings by enabling recovery of erased qudits using only a bounded number of other qudits in a repair set [18]. This notion parallels the classical ( r, δ )-LRC framework while addressing the fundamentally different structure of quantum error correction. This local recovery capability is of interest both for future large-scale quantum memories and for near-term quantum error correction. The study of qLRCs is still developing, but has seen several recent contributions. Golowich and Guruswami [18] introduced qLRCs and provided the first constructions using quantum Tamo–Barg codes, and proved a Singleton-like bound for qLRCs. Since then, many constructions of such codes have been provided in the literature [19–24]. These developments highlight ongoing progress, but explicit qLRC families derived from cyclic LRCs remain limited. 2In this work, we construct three new families of quantum ( r, δ ) locally recoverable codes derived from classical cyclic ( r, δ ) LRCs. We obtain conditions on the defining set of a cyclic code C that ensure that the dual code of C is contained in C, allowing these classical cyclic LRCs to serve as valid CSS components. Our Constructions 1 and 3 yield explicit qLRCs, which achieve the quantum Singleton-like bound for locality, whenever pure, thus giving optimal examples. Additionally, codes from Constructions 2 and 3 have no bound imposed on their lengths with respect to the field size required to obtain these codes. Moreover, to the best of our knowledge, Construction 2 is the first construction of LRCs which satisfy the afformanionted condition without imposing the condition q ≡ 1 mod r + 1. In Section 2, we present the fundamental definitions and background required for LRCs and qLRCs. Section 3 provides constructions of dual-containing cyclic LRCs of length n = q − 1 over Fq . In Section 4, we investigate dual-containing LRCs with locality r and ( r, δ ), respectively, whose lengths is not bounded by the field size required for their construction. # 2 Preliminaries 

A linear code C of length n and dimension k is a k-dimensional subspace of Fnq , and is referred to as an [ n, k ] linear code over Fq . The elements of C are known as codewords .The dual code of C, denoted by C⊥, is defined as C⊥ = {x ∈ Fnq | x·c = 0 for all c ∈ C} .It is well known that C⊥ is also a linear code with parameters [ n, n − k]. 3The Hamming distance between two codewords is the number of positions in which they differ. The minimum distance of C is the smallest Hamming distance between any two distinct codewords in C. If the minimum distance of C is d, then C is called an [ n, k, d ] code. A linear code C of length n over Fq is called cyclic if, for every codeword (c0, c 1, . . . , c n−1) ∈ C , its cyclic shift ( cn−1, c 0, . . . , c n−2) also belongs to C. By associ-ating each codeword with a polynomial c(x) = c0 + c1x + · · · + cn−1x n−1, cyclic codes can be viewed as ideals in the quotient ring Fq [x]/⟨xn − 1⟩. Since this ring is principal, every cyclic code C is generated by a unique monic divisor g(x) of xn − 1, called the generator polynomial of C. Thus, C = ⟨g(x)⟩, and dimension of C is k = n − deg g(x). An alternative description is given through the defining set, which consists of the exponents of the roots of g(x) in some extension field of Fq . This algebraic framework makes cyclic codes both theoretically appealing and practically useful.

# 5 Conclusion 

In this work, we have studied some constructions of qLRCs from classical cyclic (r, δ )-LRCs. By determining which defining sets of cyclic LRCs yield dual-containing conditions, we developed a method for constructing CSS-type qLRCs. We presented three explicit families of ( r, δ )-qLRCs, two of which attain the quantum Singleton-like bound whenever the codes are pure. Moreover, Constructions 2 and 3 admit code lengths that are unbounded with respect to the field size, which is desirable for prac-tical applications. As a direction for future work, our methods based on Theorems 7 and 9 primarily address the cases where δ = 2, and δ ≤ ⌈ L
