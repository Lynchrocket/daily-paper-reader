# Quantum Hierarchical Locally Recoverable Codes 

Venkatesan Guruswami ∗ Rutuja Kshirsagar † Pranav Trivedi ‡

Abstract 

Quantum locally recoverable codes (QLRCs) have recently gained attention as a framework for achieving efficient quantum storage with local recovery capabilities. Analogous to their classical counterparts, QLRCs allow a lost qudit to be reconstructed using only a small subset of other qudits, thereby reducing the resource and operational overhead in recovery. In this work, we extend the study of QLRCs by considering ( r, δ ) QLRCs characterized by locality parameter r and local distance δ ≥ 2. We present constructions of both random and explicit (r, δ ) QLRCs, including explicit families based on the quantum Tamo–Barg construction. We also present an efficient decoding algorithm for these quantum Tamo–Barg codes. Furthermore, we introduce quantum hierarchical locally recoverable codes (QHLRCs), which extend local recovery to multiple hierarchical levels. For any integer h ≥ 2, we construct both random and explicit h-level QHLRCs, the latter being h-level quantum Tamo–Barg codes, and establish a Singleton-like bound for these codes using a CSS framework built from dual-containing classical codes. These results advance the theoretical foundations of quantum erasure recovery and contribute to the design of efficient quantum storage architectures.

1.1 Introduction 

Quantum erasure recovery asks to reconstruct lost qudits when the erased positions are known. Local recovery is the additional requirement that each erased qudit can be reconstructed from a small neighborhood rather than from the whole code block. This locality requirement is a natural quantum analogue of classical locally recoverable codes (LRCs) [12]: it reduces the size of the recovery operation and is closely related to the locality constraints that arise in quantum LDPC code design. Erasure models are also physically motivated, since detected errors can be converted into erasures in several proposed architectures, including neutral-atom and superconducting platforms [33, 31]. The classical theory has two refinements that are especially useful in distributed storage. First, an ( r, δ )-LRC asks each local group of size at most r+δ−1 to recover any δ−1 erasures [19]. Second, a hierarchical LRC gives nested recovery groups: a small group handles common local erasures, and larger groups are used only when the smaller group is insufficient [26, 1]. This paper develops both refinements in the quantum CSS setting. We study ( r, δ ) quantum locally recoverable codes (QLRCs), introduce quantum hierarchical locally recoverable codes (QHLRCs), and give random and explicit constructions with distance bounds. The hierarchy is not just a cosmetic generalization. In the quantum setting, some stronger classical locality notions do not survive: even weak forms of availability are impossible for quantum CSS codes [11, Theorem 9]. Hierarchy is therefore a natural way to add repair flexibility while remaining compatible with quantum no-cloning constraints. Operationally, an h-level QHLRC has parameters (( r1, δ 1), . . . , (rh, δ h)) , where every coordinate lies in a top-level group correcting δ1 − 1erasures, and the punctured code on that group recursively has lower-level quantum locality. The lowest level gives the smallest recovery operation; higher levels provide fallback recovery for denser local erasure patterns. 

Relation to recent work. Golowich and Guruswami introduced QLRCs, random QLRCs, and quantum Tamo–Barg (QTB) codes for the one-erasure local case [11]. Sharma, Ramkumar, and Tamo generalized the good-polynomial approach for QLRCs and obtained distance bounds without the r +1 prime restriction appearing in the original QTB analysis [27]. Galindo, Hernando, Mart´ ın-Cruz, and Matsumoto introduced quantum ( r, δ )-LRCs, proved structural characterizations for stabilizer codes, and gave Singleton-like bounds and optimal examples [7]. Subsequent work has focused heavily on optimal or optimal pure one-level QLRCs and ( r, δ )-QLRCs, often via classical dual-containing constructions [22, 34, 21, 20, 6]. Our goal is complementary to the optimal-pure line of work. We do not claim that the explicit QTB or hierarchical QTB families meet the corresponding Singleton-like bounds in general; in fact, our examples and bounds suggest that hierarchy should be viewed primarily as improving the repair structure rather than automatically improving global distance. The advantages of the present constructions are different: they give random CSS ensembles with high-probability linear-distance guarantees, explicit algebraic families extending QTB codes from δ = 2 to arbitrary fixed 

δ, the first hierarchical quantum-locality framework in this setting, folded variants with improved asymptotic tradeoffs, and an efficient decoder for the ( r, δ )-QTB family. When h = 1 and δ = 2, the framework recovers the QTB direction of [11]; when h = 1, the hierarchical Singleton-like bound below recovers the known ( r, δ )-QLRC bound from [7]. 1Parameter regimes and structural assumptions. # 10 Discussion and Conclusions 

As demonstrated in [13, 14] quantum erasures are easier to recover than other errors. Furthermore, detected errors can be converted into erasures on quantum hardware [14, 33, 31]. Erasure recovery is also believed to aid building efficient quantum storage. To this end, we develop the theory of quantum erasure recovery by extending the study of local recovery. In particular, we extend the literature on quantum locally recoverable codes. We provide constructions of ( r, δ ) QLRCs that are CSS codes by effectively constructing the parity-check matrices of underlying classical codes. We also demonstrate explicit examples of ( r, δ ) QLRCs by constructing ( r, δ ) quantum Tamo–Barg codes. Furthermore, we explore the idea of “hierarchical local recovery” in the quantum setting. Clas-sical hierarchical locally recoverable codes were studied to improve efficiency of local recovery as well as recover more erasures. With a similar goal in mind, we define h-level quantum hierarchical locally recoverable codes for any integer h ≥ 2. In the case that h = 1, we reduce to the definition 98 of ( r, δ ) QLRCs presented in [7]. Given locality and distance parameters ( r1, δ 1), . . . , (rh, δ h), we construct h-level QHLRCs that are CSS codes. The random constructions that we present rely on carefully choosing the parity-check matrices for associated classical codes. We also present a Singleton-like bound on the minimum distance of h-level QHLRCs. When h = 1, we obtain the Singleton-like bound presented in [7]. We also present explicit constructions of h-level QHLRCs, the h-level quantum Tamo–Barg codes. In addition, we introduce folded ( r, δ ) quantum Tamo–Barg codes and folded quantum Hierarchical Tamo–Barg codes. Finally, we present an algorithm which decodes the ( r, δ ) quantum Tamo–Barg codes efficiently and can be extended to the folded codes. While this work contributes to the growing literature on quantum locally recoverable codes (QLRCs), an area that has recently attracted significant attention, several important questions remain open. The classical Tamo–Barg codes are designed such that their minimum distance achieves the Singleton-like bound for classical locally recoverable codes. In contrast, the quantum Tamo–Barg codes introduced in this work do not attain the corresponding quantum Singleton-like bound in either the QLRC or QHLRC settings. Constructing QLRCs and QHLRCs that meet their respective quantum Singleton-like bounds therefore remains an open problem. Moreover, devising new proof techniques to prove tighter lower bounds on the minimum distances derived in this work is also of interest. Finally, a key avenue for future exploration lies in understanding the practical implications of these theoretical advances—specifically, how such quantum codes can be effectively integrated into emerging quantum hardware architectures.
