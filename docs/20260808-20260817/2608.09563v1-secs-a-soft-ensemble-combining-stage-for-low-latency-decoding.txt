# SECS: A Soft Ensemble-Combining Stage for Low-Latency Decoding 

Felix Krieg †, Paul Bezner †, and Stephan ten Brink 

Institute of Telecommunications, University of Stuttgart, Germany 

{krieg, bezner, tenbrink }@inue.uni-stuttgart.de 

Abstract —Ensemble decoding is a promising technique for ultra-reliable low-latency communication, as it trades hardware parallelism for decoding latency by running M diverse belief propagation (BP) decoders in parallel. Conventional ensembles select their output from the member candidates. Hence, decoding fails whenever no member finds the correct codeword. In this work, we show that shallow, diverse BP members are poor 

correctors , but excellent sorters . Based on this observation, we propose a soft ensemble-combining stage (SECS) that com-bines extrinsic messages after only a few iterations, yielding a reliability ordering whose most reliable positions are nearly error-free. A subsequent re-encoding stage (like ordered-statistics decoding (OSD)) converts this ordering into a near-maximum likelihood (ML) candidate codeword, at a fraction of the BP latency of a fully converged ensemble. We demonstrate the proposed scheme on three short codes: a (63 , 30) Bose–Ray-Chaudhuri–Hocquenghem code, an overcomplete PG(2 , 8) code, and a search-designed (105 , 53) cyclic code. In all cases, the SECS with OSD post-processing closes most of the gap to ML decoding, while significantly reducing the number of required BP iterations. 

Index Terms —URLLC, short block codes, ensemble decoding, belief propagation, ordered statistics. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Ultra-reliable low-latency communication (URLLC) targets error probabilities as low as 10 −5 within a 1 ms user-plane latency budget [1]. Such stringent latency and reliability requirements necessitate short codes that can be decoded close to maximum likelihood (ML) performance. For short, dense codes, iterative BP decoding on a single graph often saturates well before reaching ML performance. Ensemble decoding addresses this limitation by operating M

decoders in parallel on diverse representations of the same code and returning the most likely valid candidate through an ML-selection rule. Diversity stems, e.g., from multiple parity-check bases in multiple-bases belief propagation (MBBP) [2] or from automorphism-induced permutations in automorphism ensemble decoding (AED) [3]. A conventional selection-only ensemble is bounded by its list error rate (LER), defined as the probability that none of its constituent decoders produces the transmitted codeword.

No selection rule can recover such a frame from the mem-ber candidate list. Surpassing this bound therefore requires synthesizing an additional candidate from information that is distributed across the ensemble members. This paper starts from a simple observation: shallow, di-verse BP members correct little, but they sort exceptionally well, as shown in Fig. 1. Based on this observation, we propose the soft ensemble-combining stage (SECS). After only a few iterations, SECS aggregates the clipped extrinsic messages of the ensemble members into a combined LLR vec-tor whose reliability ordering concentrates the residual errors in the least reliable positions. A re-encoding post-processing – low-order ordered-statistics decoding (OSD) [4] or a variant without Gaussian elimination (GE) – then recovers the code-word from the nearly error-free most reliable positions. In the studied configurations, deeper BP decoding generally reduces the average BER, but also flattens the ordered-reliability error profile. Consequently, its remaining errors can occur inside the most-reliable basis (MRB), making the posterior less suitable for re-encoding even when its average BER is lower. Feeding BP soft output to OSD is a classical idea [5]. Prior work improves the OSD input through temporal accumula-tion and decoder diversity. MBBP-X [2] combines soft output across parity-check bases, but feeds it back into the constituent decoders. In contrast, we fuse clipped extrinsic information across ensemble members at a common shallow iteration and apply a re-encoding to the resulting reliability vector. The proposed method therefore synthesizes a candidate from information distributed across several decoding trajectories, rather than ap-plying post-processing separately to member-wise or iteration-wise soft outputs. This distinction is particularly relevant for short, dense graphs, where individual BP posteriors frequently contain erroneous bits with saturated reliability. Shallow cross-member fusion suppresses such member-specific errors while preserving the steep reliability ordering required by low-order OSD. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:  

> •

We propose the SECS, which combines the member ex-trinsics of a BP ensemble after only a few iterations and passes the result to a re-encoding stage. The combining weight is optimized for each studied ensemble.  

> •

We analyze the soft-combining gain via ordered-reliability error profiles and derive a closed-form frame error rate (FER) estimate from them.  

> •

We compare the proposed SECS to selection-based en-semble decoding on three codes and identify the regimes in which combining is beneficial.  

> •

We describe a search procedure for cyclic codes with many low-weight dual codewords. Plain BP fails to decode the resulting (105 , 53) code, while OSD with SECS input operates close to ML.  

> •

We present an alternative post-processing decoder. It can be interpreted as an OSD whose re-encoding stage is computed by BP hardware, enabling OSD-like perfor-mance where a full OSD is not affordable. Fig. 2 shows the resulting SECS-based architecture. M par-allel BP decoders run for a few iterations only. Instead of continuing as separate decoders, the SECS (blue border) aggregates their extrinsics. The combined LLRs are then passed to a re-encoding stage (in blue). Optionally, a post-processing step (in orange) prepares the combined LLRs as input for another round of classical ensemble decoding (in gray). Alternatively, the BP ensemble itself can be used to mimic the re-encoding step of an OSD. II. P RELIMINARIES AND SYSTEM MODEL

In multiple-bases ensembles [2], the members use structurally different sparse PCMs of the same code. For the BCH codes, we employ structured sparse parity-check matrix (ssPCM) constructions [9], [10], in which each dense dual row is decomposed into several low-weight checks, coupled through punctured auxiliary variable nodes (AVNs). For codes with redundant duals, we draw independent rank-preserving row subsets of an overcomplete PCM. In automor-phism ensembles [3], a single matrix is used under coordinate permutations from the automorphism group of the code. The finite-geometry codes considered below combine both views: their duals contain the incidence vectors of all lines of the underlying geometry [11]. These form an overcomplete PCM that can be sparsified and permuted [12]. Each ensemble member m runs flooding BP and stops after 

Imax iterations. Its extrinsic message vector on the n code bits is em. For ssPCM members, the SECS combines the posteriors of the n code-bit variable nodes only; the punctured AVNs (initialized to zero, without channel LLRs) are never combined, and em is the code-bit posterior minus Lch . Single-iteration members employ one round of the normalized min– sum algorithm (NMSA), whereas members with multiple iterations use sum–product algorithm (SPA) updates [13], [14]. Both are attenuated by a factor α. NMSA uses α = 0 .75 ,SPA uses α = 0 .5. Single-iteration members use NMSA. Its suboptimal approximation cannot propagate within one iteration. Multi-iteration members, in contrast, use the exact SPA update, damped for stability on the loopy, dense graphs. As a reference post-processing, we use OSD-1 [4]: the input positions are sorted by reliability, the MRB is obtained by GE, and the decision is the highest-correlation candidate among the re-encoded words of order at most one, i.e., all single-bit flips on the MRB. A. BCH (63 , 30) 

We first consider the (63 , 30) BCH code [17], [18], decoded with an ssPCM ensemble (see Sec. II) [10]. Fig. 6a compares the ssPCM ensemble ( M =16 , tapped after Imax =2 iterations) against single-decoder and hard-out ensemble baselines (with ML selection). At this low iteration count, the hard decision on the SECS output already outperforms the ensemble with ML selection. The larger gain, however, comes from the post-processing on the ordered LLRs: OSD-1 on Lcomb gains roughly two decades over OSD-1 on a single BP posterior and approaches the ML performance of the BCH code. At this rate, OSD performance is limited by the quality of its ordering. The combined ordering is substantially better than both the posterior of a converged single BP decoder and the ML-selected output of a converged ensemble. 

B. PG(2,8) 

The (73 , 45) projective-geometry code admits an overcom-plete PCM of 73 lines. A rank-preserving (non-overcomplete) part of it, permuted under the automorphism group, yields eight AED ensemble members. Fig. 6b shows the resulting FER performance. We combine the eight members after a sin-gle min-sum iteration, followed by OSD-1. This outperforms the same members run to ten iterations with ML selection, at one tenth of the iteration latency. Up to 4 dB, it also tracks the channel OSD-3 reference closely. The strongest single-decoder baseline is BP-10 on the full overcomplete matrix, which, even with OSD-1 post-processing, cannot reach the performance of the SECS. 

C. A Search-Designed (105 , 53) Code 

The preceding results raise the question whether codes can be constructed specifically for this decoder. Its main requirement is a rich supply of low-weight parity checks. We searched cyclic codes of length 63 –105 (rate 0.40 –0.75 )for abundant low-weight dual orbits : in a cyclic code, every dual codeword of weight w contributes its full n-shift orbit of weight-w checks. Some codes offer good dual orbits, but poor distance properties. If xd −1 divides the generator polynomial, light dual orbits are plentiful, but the code inherits low-weight codewords. Hence, we verify the minimum distance of every candidate by information-set decoding. The strongest survivor is a (105 , 53) code with dmin =10 ,below the best known 18 for these parameters. D. OSD from BP Hardware 

The pipeline of Fig. 2 can also be read as an OSD whose expensive stages are realized by BP hardware. A conventional OSD-1 implementation requires reliability sorting, a per-frame GE to determine the MRB, and k + 1 re-encodings. The data-dependent GE can impose a complexity and latency floor. This has motivated adaptive methods that reduce or omit it whenever possible [19]. For BCH codes, Yang et al. [20] avoid online GE by exploiting the Reed–Solomon parent-code structure to generate candidates from systematic generator matrices. In contrast, our GE-free variant uses the available BP decoder for candidate completion. It is not limited to algebraic constructions, but does benefit from the cyclicity of the code. The most effective follow-up stage to the SECS that we found is a rebuild of OSD-1 itself from the available BP decoder, needing neither the GE nor the re-encodings. The

We introduced the SECS, a combining stage for BP ensem-ble decoders that fuses the clipped extrinsics of all members after one or two iterations and passes the combined LLR to a post-processing stage. The stage exploits a simple property of unconverged BP: shallow, diverse members correct little, but sort exceptionally well. On all three studied codes, the SECS with OSD-1 operates within small factors of the ML reference and outperforms ten-iteration selection ensembles at one fifth to one tenth of the iteration latency. The gain requires genuine graph diversity; its source – automorphisms, multiple bases, or ssPCM constructions – is secondary. Optimized low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes offer little such diversity: their only light dual codewords are the designed rows, so same-graph ensembles built by per-turbation share most failure events and gain correspondingly little from combining. The ordered-reliability profile and the estimator (3) identify the favorable regime – sloped per-bit BER and no BP overconfidence plateau. The designed (105 , 53) code shows that the diversity re-quirement is constructive. Trading minimum distance 18 for 

10 buys ten weight-8 dual orbits, and the resulting code decodes within 2–2.5× of its ML performance at a single iteration, while the best known code of equal parameters leaves its superior distance locked behind a complex decoding challenge. Additionally, with help of an OSD-like post-processing whose ordering and re-encoding are supplied by BP hardware, the complete pipeline requires no GE. Future work includes automorphism-based member con-struction for designed codes, design searches at longer block lengths, and a hardware elaboration of the single-iteration combining datapath.
