Abstract 

Clifford codes are a natural generalization of quantum stabilizer codes based primarily on representa-tion theory. This class of codes has previously been extended to the setting of quantum subsystem codes. We formulate a two-fold generalization of Clifford codes, for both the hybrid classical and quantum infor-mation and projective representation theory settings. This leads to new classes of hybrid subspace and subsystem Clifford codes. We extend the fundamental representation theoretic quantum error correction theorem to include these codes, based on the operator algebra quantum error correction framework. We also discuss several examples throughout the presentation, of both stabilizer and non-stabilizer type. 

# 1 Introduction 

Quantum error correction has grown over the past three decades to become both central to the ongoing and continued development of quantum technologies, and a core area in quantum information theory [31, 37]. Recent high-profile advances in the quantum computing industry have been based on several important factors, with a strong foundation of theoretical quantum error correction being one of them. Future advances will surely be built on additional theoretical advances, and so there are compelling reasons to broaden and deepen the theory further, including in quantum error correction. Historically, the most important class of quantum codes has been the class of stabilizer codes [15, 16], originally build for error models made up of operators from the Pauli group. An early generalization of these codes extended the formulation of stabilizer codes based on a group representation theory approach, and this led to what are now called Clifford codes [21,22,24,25]. Subsequently, the original class of Clifford codes were generalized to a more exotic type of quantum code called subsystem codes [20,23], which have been intensely investigated over the past two decades [1, 27, 39]. In a different direction, including more recent work, error correction approaches have been developed for hybrid classical and quantum codes [5,13,17–19,30,32,34,35], which have a strong connection with the theory of operator algebras [4, 9, 29]. Of particular relevance to the present work, we mention the operator algebra quantum error correction framework [2, 3], and the recent extension of the stabilizer formalism to a setting that includes hybrid (subspace and subsystem) codes [10, 33]. Taken together, these earlier and more recent motivations suggest an extension of the class of Clifford codes to hybrid and operator algebra error correction settings would be worthwhile and should be possible. In this paper, we introduce such an extension of Clifford codes, one that embraces hybrid versions of the codes and more directly links them with operator algebra theory. Furthermore, our generalization is two-fold, as we introduce another new element into the study of these codes: projective representation theory,

# 2 Preliminaries 

In quantum coding theory [26, 31, 37], one is typically interested in finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces H

made up of multiple qubit (i.e., C2) or qudit (i.e., Cd with d ≥ 2) subsystems, and so H = ( Cd)⊗n. This is called n-qubit or n-qudit Hilbert space, and we denote the standard (computational) orthonormal basis by {| i1 · · · id⟩ : 0 ≤ ij ≤ d − 1}, with the convention |i1 · · · id⟩ := |i1⟩ ⊗ · · · ⊗ | id⟩. Quantum codes are usually identified with subspaces of a quantum system Hilbert space, with generalized notions defined by a subsystem of a subspace (for subsystem codes), or a collection of orthogonal subspaces (for hybrid codes). We will introduce the specific code families and the relevant theoretical aspects of quantum error correction for our investigation in the sections that follow; here we simply include some introductory notes. The original formulation of Clifford codes [21, 22, 24, 25] in quantum error correction came from a group representation theory motivated generalization of stabilizer codes [15, 16], which are historically the most important class of quantum codes. Subsequently, they were generalized to subsystem code constructions [20, 23], which is an important extension of regular (subspace) quantum codes that have grown in importance over the past two decades [1, 27, 39]. 2.1 Projective Representation Theory 

As projective representation theory has only recently been considered in a quantum error correction context [14], for the rest of this section we will review key elements from the theory (for further details see [6] as well). We have also included a remark below on the use of this theory in quantum error correction. 

Definition 1. Let G be a finite group. A projective representation of G on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space H is a function π : G → U (H), where U(H) is the unitary group on H, such that the composition 

q ◦ π : G → P U(H) is a group homomorphism, where P U(H) is the quotient of U(H) by scalar multiples of the identity and q : U(H) → P U(H) is the quotient map. We say that π is projectively faithful if the composition q ◦ π is injective. 

Remark. The reason it is desirable to work with projective representations in quantum information is as follows. To implement measurements in quantum mechanics one must compute various inner products 

⟨φ|ψ⟩. This quantity remains unchanged if we change |ψ⟩ and |φ⟩ by the same global phase, i.e. if we act by a unimodular scalar multiple of the identity on H. Thus, all the unimodular scalar multiples of the identity are irrelevant information for us to remember, in other words, the physics we want to describe is completely captured by the group homomorphism q ◦ π : G → P U(H).A natural question might be, why do we not simply work with these group homomorphisms to P U(H)

directly? The answer is that this does not constitute a good representation theory, certain theoretical results simply do not hold for such group homomorphisms, but they do hold for projective representations. An example of such a phenomenon is highlighted in [14, Section 3], where it is shown that it is possible to have inequivalent projective representations π1 and π2 such that q ◦ π1 = q ◦ π2.That the composition q ◦ π in Definition 1 is a group homomorphism is equivalent to the existence of a function σ : G × G → T, where T is the unit circle in the complex plane, such that 

π(x)π(y) = σ(x, y )π(xy ), for all x, y ∈ G. (1) We note that this differs slightly from the definition in [6] where the σ-function is conjugated. One can readily verify from Eq. (1) that σ satisfies the following cocycle equation for all x, y, z ∈ G:

σ(x, y )σ(xy, z ) = σ(x, yz )σ(y, z ), (2) the function σ is therefore aptly named a cocycle . We will often use the notation σπ to denote the cocycle associated to the projective representation π. Write the identity element of G as e and note that σ(x, e )

# 4 Hybrid Clifford Subspace and Subsystem Codes 

In this section we formulate our class of Clifford codes, with subclasses defined sequentially as extra features are added in the discussion. Reflecting one of our generalization directions, let us first briefly review key elements of the recent construction of hybrid subspace and subsystem codes from [10] based on the operator algebra quantum error correction approach. In [10], codes are constructed from the following algebraic data of the Pauli group Pn. The initial data is the group of stabilizers, S, which is an abelian subgroup of Pn that does not contain −I. From this one considers a splitting of the normalizer group N (S) into a cartesian product N (S) ≃ L × G . The group L

is the group of (bare) logical operators, and G is the so-called gauge group. Then, let T ⊂ P n be a choice of representatives of the (left) cosets Pn/N (S), a so-called coset transversal, and let T0 ⊆ T be a subset. A code C = C(S, G, L, T 0) is then defined to be the following subspace of H = ( C2)⊗n,

# 6 Conclusion 

In this work, we have added to the library of quantum error correcting codes, by extending the theory of Clifford codes to the setting of hybrid subspace and subsystem codes. Our generalization was two-fold, in that we have used it to inject projective representation theory more directly into the subject, and we have built it upon the operator algebra quantum error correction framework. There are a number of potential lines of investigation coming out of this work. Within the class of codes we have introduced themselves, one can ask if there are extensions of other results from Clifford code theory, such as a representation theoretic description of which ones are stabilizer codes. We have included subsystem codes in our formulation, and we noted how one can construct them in our examples, but we have not investigated their properties deeply. We introduced a notion of code distance for these codes, but, as in, it seems, other Clifford code settings, we have not investigated it in detail. This is a direction to pursue for specific code subclasses of interest in applications, with the recent interest in subsystem codes as an example. One could also ask if there are natural entanglement-assisted versions of these codes [12, 33]. The operator algebra framework allows for a more natural mix of the various hybrid and non-hybrid code types, and, additionally, it invites consideration of infinite-dimensional generalizations of Clifford codes. We leave these and related investigations for elsewhere.
